# 07 — V0.2R: Causal truth → Credit representation

$$\boxed{\text{Given that we know what happened, \emph{what should be changed?}}}$$

**This version has no online learning loop.** It compares *representations of
where change should occur*, scored directly against the evaluator's repair truth.

---

## 1. Why there is no learning loop here

The natural design would be: fix some update primitive, vary the credit unit,
and pick the unit with the better learning curve. That design is rejected, and
the reason is the central methodological correction of the rebuild.

If the fixed primitive — say `NegativeOnly` — is itself poor, then

$$R_A + \texttt{NegativeOnly} \;\;>\;\; R_B + \texttt{NegativeOnly}$$

does **not** establish that $R_A$ is the better representation. The winner may be
decided entirely by a $Unit \times Primitive$ interaction. The legacy project made
exactly this mistake in a different form: it compared arms whose differences were
produced by a hidden assumption rather than by the factor under study.

$$\boxed{\text{Do not select a representation with an unvalidated primitive.}}$$

So V0.2R removes the primitive from the loop entirely and asks a purely
representational question, answerable without any training:

> Which credit representation most faithfully expresses *where the change should
> occur*?

The primitive's turn comes in V0.3R, where the representation is **fixed to the
V0.2R winner**.

---

## 2. Truth being predicted

The repair truth, not the cause truth (`02-SCM.md` §4):

$$R^{*} = \text{the minimal sufficient intervention set},\qquad |R^{*}| \text{ and } \#R^{*} \text{ reported separately}$$

V0.1R predicted $C$ (what is broken). V0.2R predicts $R^{*}$ (what must change).
These are different objects and the distinction is what makes the two versions
separable.

---

## 3. The competing representations

Each representation is a **schema**: a rule that maps an episode to a set of
candidate credit units.

| representation | unit | granularity |
|---|---|---|
| $R_{\text{module}}$ | H/L module | coarse; the legacy ontology, now merely a candidate |
| $R_{\text{trajectory}}$ | the whole episode | coarsest; "something in this run" |
| $R_{\text{causal}}$ | Plan / Decision$_t$ / Execution$_t$ / Process | the rebuild's causal factorisation |
| $R_{\text{repair}}$ | the minimal sufficient intervention set itself | finest; the truth, as a representation |

$R_{\text{repair}}$ is the target expressed as a representation. It is included as
a **reference ceiling**, not as a competitor to be adopted — no method may consult
the evaluator's enumeration, so its scores bound what any representation could
achieve, not what a method can.

H/L is **not banned**. It competes on equal terms. If it loses on the endpoints
below, it is removed from the rebuild. If it wins, it is adopted and the earlier
assumption is vindicated — either outcome is informative, which is the whole
point of demoting it from axiom to candidate.

---

## 4. Endpoints

Each representation induces a set of proposed units $\hat R$. All endpoints are
computed against $R^{*}$.

| endpoint | definition | role |
|---|---|---|
| **InterventionSufficiency** | fraction of episodes where applying $\hat R$ (via a fixed canonical conversion rule) actually makes the episode succeed | **primary** |
| **FalseEditRate** | fraction of proposed units that are **not** in any sufficient set | **co-primary** |
| $R^{*}$-Coverage | fraction of the true minimal set's members that are proposed | secondary |
| SitePrecision | proposed units that are in $R^{*}$ | secondary |
| SiteRecall | members of $R^{*}$ that are proposed | secondary |
| CandidateSetSize | mean $\#\hat R$ | secondary — a representation that proposes everything scores well on recall and is useless |
| Tie handling | does the representation admit multiple candidates when $\#R^{*} > 1$? | **structural, pass/fail** |

### 4.1 Why sufficiency and false-edit rate are co-primary

Recall alone is gamed by proposing everything; precision alone is gamed by
proposing nothing. The pair is the smallest set of numbers that resists both.
`InterventionSufficiency` answers *"would this have fixed it?"*;
`FalseEditRate` answers *"what did it break on the way?"* — and the second is the
endpoint the legacy project was blind to until `WithinModuleDamage` was invented.

### 4.2 The canonical conversion rule

Converting a representation into an intervention set requires a rule, and the
rule must be **identical across representations** and frozen. Otherwise the
comparison measures the conversion, not the representation. It is declared in the
preregistration and applied mechanically.

---

## 5. The secondary probe, and its hard restriction

Every representation additionally undergoes the *same* blunt probe: apply
$-\delta$ to the units it proposes, and measure damage.

$$\text{WithinUnitDamage}, \quad \text{CrossUnitCollateral}, \quad \text{EditCount}$$

**Restriction:**

$$\boxed{\text{The probe's task-utility AUC may \textbf{not} be used to select the V0.3R representation.}}$$

The probe is reported because it is cheap and diagnostic. It is not allowed to
decide, because a blunt constant edit interacts with the representation for
reasons that have nothing to do with whether the representation is faithful.

---

## 6. The interaction guard (end of V0.3R, not here)

After V0.3R identifies the best primitive, a small bridge experiment runs

$$2\ \text{representations} \times 2\ \text{primitives}$$

for one purpose only: to confirm that the V0.2R winner has not been completely
overturned by a representation × primitive interaction. It is **not** a re-selection
of the representation, and it carries no primary hypothesis.

If the bridge finds a strong interaction, that is a finding in its own right — it
would mean credit representations cannot be ranked independently of update rules,
which would be an important negative result about the whole research programme.

---

## 7. Not in this version

* no policy learning, no Q updates, no learning curves;
* no learned attribution — V0.2R receives **cause truth** $C$, because V0.1R
  already owns the inference question and mixing them would re-entangle the chain;
* no candidate scoring $\hat V(c) - \lambda U(c) - \beta C_{\text{switch}}(c)$ —
  that is V0.4R.

---

## 8. Go / no-go

| gate | condition |
|---|---|
| Identifiability Gate E (`03` §2) | PASS — $R^{*}$ well defined for every generated episode |
| Semantic suite (`04`) | PASS — in particular **C7** (ties reported as size-1, two candidates) and **C4** (genuine process faults) |
| Development calibration | the four representations produce *different* $\hat R$ on a non-trivial fraction of episodes; if they agree everywhere the version is uninformative |

**If V0.2R fails** — meaning no representation reliably proposes a sufficient,
low-collateral set even with cause truth given — then the problem is not
attribution and not the update rule. The conclusion is that *the notion of a
credit unit this project is using is wrong*, and the programme should stop and
rethink the representation before building anything on top of it.
